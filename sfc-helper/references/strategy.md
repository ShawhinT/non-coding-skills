## Who Shaw sells to (the avatar)

AI Builder Academy sells AI enablement / transformation to **50–200-person, English-operating, PE/VC-backed, financial-services-adjacent tech companies** (cyber, fintech, SaaS, investment firms). The buyer is a **two-person committee**:

- **Sponsor** — CEO / founder / Chairman, or CFO / COO. Triggers on **status/FOMO** ("we're behind," "not maximizing it"), closes fast, often *not* hands-on, delegates execution. This is who reach posts are for.
- **Champion / rollout lead** — the person tasked with the rollout (ops or transformation lead, MD, function head). Cares about **how**: governance, adoption, proof of impact. Controls how far the engagement expands. This is who authority posts are for, and who the AI Rollout Audit CTA targets.

**Governance and data security are the universal gate** on every enterprise deal — and the most underused content territory. The free **AI Rollout Audit** is the standing CTA; frame it at the champion but write it to calm the sponsor's "what if our data leaks" fear.

## The barbell content model

Put weight on two ends, starve the mediocre middle.

- **Sponsor posts (reach end):** status, FOMO, strategic risk. e.g. "the gap between people who maximize AI and those who don't," "most founders are barely scratching the surface," business-model disruption takes.
- **Champion posts (authority end):** governance ("deploy without leaking data"), the skill-building method, **quantified outcomes** ("10 hrs/wk → 1").
- **Reach satellite (~1/week):** high-variance, ICP-flavored curiosity or emotion. Broad *within the buyer's world*, never a generic meme that pulls students and other creators.
- **Kill the middle:** abstract analogies aimed at no one (e.g. "rails vs streets"). They get neither reach nor engagement.

## Teach what you sell (and where ideas come from)

Three principles absorbed from Uptown's LinkedIn revenue system (the outbound agency behind the Uptown method in conversion-copy) — they slot cleanly into the champion/authority end of the barbell:

- **Teach exactly how to do the thing you sell.** DIYers take the free lesson and self-serve — they were never buyers. People who'd rather pay raise their hands, warmer for having seen the expertise demonstrated. Depth is the qualifier, not the giveaway; this is the *why* behind quantified-outcome and method posts.
- **Mine sales calls for topics.** The best-performing post ideas are the questions prospects actually asked and the aha moments from discovery calls — pull from the last ~3 months of call transcripts. This is the same well the `qa_pairs` mine-file feeds from; call transcripts are a first-class source for refilling it.
- **Content and outbound compound.** Outreach targeted at *active* LinkedIn users (people who post) means new connections actually see and engage with Shaw's content — and content, in turn, makes his name familiar before the DM lands. The goal of posting is not virality; it's that a prospect already half-knows Shaw when outbound reaches them. (The outbound half lives in the `outreach` skill.)

**Two more topic wells beyond calls.** *Old lessons:* something Shaw lived years ago (PhD, Toyota, the dealership, early SaaS builds), told as the moment and the lesson without the backstory, and tied to a problem the buyer has now. *News:* a specific event from the last few days plus Shaw's firsthand take or test ("Anthropic shipped X yesterday. I ran it on my CRM."). Naming the event and putting Shaw in the first line is what separates this from the abstract trend opener that flops. Its value decays daily, so it ships within a day or two rather than joining the backlog.

## What the data says (recurring patterns)

- **Reach and engagement rate are nearly decoupled.** The widest-reach post is often the lowest-rate. So a reach play doesn't cost engagement quality — but pick *qualified* reach (ICP emotion/status), not random virality.
- **The best single archetype is broad-hook + real substance** — it wins on reach *and* rate. Make it the heaviest slice.
- **Comments are the #1 lever** — posts that draw comments pull far ahead on distribution.
- **Losers:** feature listicles, abstract analogies, off-thesis topics, generic memes.

## Cadence and sequencing

**Laying out a week lives in `references/scheduling-principles.md`** — volume, reach/engagement alternation, tee-up spacing, format, CTA handling, and what earns a slot. Read it before proposing or rescheduling a week; don't re-derive cadence from this file.

Two things that changed and are worth naming so old advice doesn't resurface: reach posts are **alternated** through the week rather than front-loaded Mon–Wed (the priming effect works pairwise, not as a block), and there is **no soft CTA** — a post either carries an ask or it doesn't.

## The "what separates winners" lens (for the brainstorm gap-check)

When generating and pruning, contrast against what's worked: belief/POV + substance and status/FOMO openers win; abstract analogies, feature roundups, and off-thesis posts sink.

The sponsor/champion mix is a judgment about what a given week is doing, not a count. What does hold per-post: **one post, one role** — a post targets the sponsor or the champion, never both.
