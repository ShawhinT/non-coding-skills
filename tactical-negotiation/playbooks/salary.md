# Playbook: Salary / Raise Negotiation

Goal: move the number without triggering a "cost" frame. You want them to see
you as an investment, and you want their range before you commit to yours.

## Sequence
1. **Get their range first (label, don't ask).**
   > "It sounds like you guys already have a range in mind."
   Let them fill the silence. A label pulls the number out more often than a
   direct question, and without you anchoring low.
2. **Reframe from pay to value (calibrated question).**
   > "How can I be involved in the projects that are most critical to the team
   > this year?"
   This makes you needed. Indispensable people are cheaper to pay than to lose.
3. **If they anchor low, don't argue — make them solve it.**
   > "How am I supposed to make that work given [market / my offers / the scope]?"
   > "What would it take to get to [target]?"
4. **Use loss framing if they're assertive.** Emphasize what the team loses by
   under-leveling you, not what you'd gain.
5. **Close warm, door open.**
   > "I really appreciate how openly you're talking this through with me."

## Highest-leverage lines
- "It sounds like you guys already have a range in mind." *(label)*
- "How can I be involved in the projects critical to the company's future?"
- "What would you need to see from me to get to [number]?"
- "How am I supposed to accept that given [competing offer / market]?"
- "You probably had strong candidates — what made you pick me?" *(rebuilds your value)*

## Traps
- Don't name your number first if you can pull theirs out with a label.
- Don't justify your ask with need ("I have bills"); justify with value and
  market.
- Don't accept on the call if you're unsure — "Let me sit with this" is fine.
