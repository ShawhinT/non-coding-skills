# Playbook: Vendor / Price Pushback

Goal: stop defending your number and find the real constraint. Price-hammering
usually means you're being treated as a competing bid — reframe to value and
fit, or protect the implementation ("how") instead of the number.

## Sequence
1. **Label the value gap, don't defend.**
   > "It sounds like the value just isn't there for you at this number."
   Let them correct you. Often the objection isn't price — it's risk, timing, or
   an internal comparison.
2. **Find who you're up against (calibrated question).**
   > "What else would make this a good deal for you besides the price?"
   > "What are we up against here?"
3. **Hand back an impossible discount as their problem.**
   > "How am I supposed to do that and still deliver [the quality/scope you need]?"
4. **Protect the 'how.'** If they push hard on price, trade on terms, scope, or
   timeline — not just the number. A cheap deal you can't deliver is a loss.
   > "If we moved on price, what would you be able to flex on — timeline, scope,
   > commitment?"
5. **Anchor to something objective (bring out the robot).**
   > "It seems like the market sets this range, not either of us."
6. **Close warm even if unresolved.**
   > "Even if we can't land this today, I'd want nothing more than to keep the
   > door open."

## Highest-leverage lines
- "It sounds like the value just isn't there for you." *(label)*
- "What else would make this a good deal for you besides price?"
- "How am I supposed to make that work and still deliver what you need?"
- "What are we up against here?" *(surfaces the competing bid)*
- "What assurances can you give to make that work for me?" *(if they want terms)*

## Traps
- Don't itemize-justify the price — it invites line-by-line haggling.
- Don't discount without getting something back (scope, term, timeline, referral).
- Don't let a single number distract you from an unworkable implementation.
