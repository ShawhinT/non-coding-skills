# Playbook: Repairing Blown Trust

Goal: when something went wrong and the other side has gone cold or angry, defuse
before you fix. Name the accusation before they do, apologize clean, then hand
them the repair. Excuses re-inflate the anger — resist them.

## Sequence
1. **Accusation audit — say the worst thing out loud first.**
   > "You feel like I've failed you."
   > "This probably feels like a slap in the face."
   Naming it takes the charge out of it.
2. **Pause. Let it be uncomfortable.** Don't rush to defend. Silence signals you
   can sit with it.
3. **Apologize clean — no excuse attached.**
   > "I'm sorry."
   The moment you add "but here's what happened," you cancel the apology.
4. **Hand them the repair (calibrated question).**
   > "What would I need to do to start making this right?"
   > "How do we move on from here?"
5. **Rebuild forward, not backward.** Agree on how you'll catch the next problem
   early:
   > "How do we know if this goes off track again, and what do we do if it does?"

## Highest-leverage lines
- "You feel like I've failed you." *(accusation audit)*
- "It feels like I haven't earned back your trust yet." *(surfaces the real state)*
- "I'm sorry." *(clean — full stop)*
- "What would I need to do to start making this right?"
- "How do we make sure this doesn't happen again?"

## Traps
- No "but," no explanation-disguised-as-apology.
- Don't over-promise to buy back trust fast — commit to the repair process, not
  a miracle.
- Don't skip the silence; rushing past the discomfort reads as not meaning it.
