# Playbook: Declining Without Wrecking the Relationship

Goal: say no clearly, keep the warmth, and leave the door open. The whole game
is separating the appreciation from the decline with a clean stop — no "but."

## Sequence
1. **Appreciate, genuinely (admiration audit).**
   > "This is genuinely generous, and I really respect how you put it together."
2. **Decline cleanly. Full stop. No 'but.'**
   > "I'm sorry — it just doesn't work for me."
   The moment you say "but," you erase the appreciation. Use a period.
3. **Keep it open (calibrated question).**
   > "What am I missing that made this the right number for you?"
   > "What would need to change for this to work down the line?"
4. **Reaffirm the relationship.**
   > "Even if we don't do this one, I'm rooting for you and want to keep the door
   > open."

## If you might still deal
Swap the flat no for a softener + counter:
> "You're probably not going to love this…" (pause) "…here's what I could
> actually do."

## Highest-leverage lines
- "Your offer is very generous — I'm sorry, that doesn't work for me."
- "I'm afraid I just can't do that."
- "I don't see how that would ever work." *(harder wake-up)*
- "Even if we don't get a deal done here and now, I'm always going to be rooting
  for you."

## Traps
- No "but," no "however," no "unfortunately-but." Stop after the no.
- Don't over-explain the reason — one clean line beats a paragraph of excuses.
- Don't apologize your way into reopening a deal you've decided against.
