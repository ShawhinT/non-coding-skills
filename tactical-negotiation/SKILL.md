# Tactical Negotiation

Help the user say the right thing in a negotiation or high-stakes conversation.
The method is **tactical empathy**: you win by making the other person feel
deeply understood, not by out-arguing them. Your job is to (1) read the moment,
(2) pick the right move, and (3) produce natural, human phrasing the user can
say or send.

## How to use this skill

1. Get the situation. What was said, by whom, what the user wants, and the
   relationship stakes. If you're missing the counterpart's actual words, ask
   for them — the exact language is what you mirror and label.
2. Choose a move using the selection procedure below.
3. Deliver phrasing. Draft it in the user's voice, keep it warm, and end on a
   positive. When you need the precise wording, pull from
   `references/phrasebook.md`.
4. For a recognizable scenario (raise, vendor/price, declining, missing
   stakeholders, blown trust), open the matching file in `playbooks/` — it
   sequences the moves and lists the highest-leverage lines for that context.

## Core principles

These are the beliefs behind every move. Reason from them; don't just pattern-match.

1. **Emotion is contagious and pre-verbal.** People read *how you feel* before
   they process *what you say*. Lead with calm, warmth, curiosity, and genuine
   good will — it transfers to them. So does anxiety and irritation, so manage
   yours.
2. **Never argue — redirect.** When you hit resistance, do exactly one of three
   things instead of countering: state what *you* feel, label *their* situation,
   or ask a calibrated question. Arguing hardens positions; these open them.
3. **Talk to System 1.** Aim for fast, unguarded, emotional reactions, not
   reasoned defenses. Use questions to *shape thought*, not to gather facts.
4. **"What" is nothing without "How."** A yes you have to drag them through is
   worthless. Get them to articulate *how* it works — better yet, make the
   solution feel like *their* idea. People execute their own ideas.
5. **Make it feel fair, always.** Give a standing invitation to be called out
   for unfairness. Fairness is the emotional floor of any durable deal.
6. **End on a positive, every time.** Whatever the outcome, close on an up-note.
   It protects the relationship and the option value of a future deal.
7. **Silence is a tool.** Pauses surface information and signal control. After a
   label or a hard line, stop talking and let it land.

## The move-selection procedure

When the user hits friction, walk this decision path:

- **They're emotional, guarded, or something's clearly off** → lead with a
  **label** ("It seems like…", "It sounds like…", "It feels like…"). Name the
  emotion to drain it. Try a label *before* any question.
- **You want them to keep talking / reveal more** → **mirror** their last few
  words back with upward inflection, then go silent.
- **You need movement, a concession, or to hand them a problem** → **calibrated
  question** ("How am I supposed to…?", "What would you need to make this
  work?"). "How" and "what" put the burden on them without a fight.
- **You need a firm boundary or a decision** → **no-oriented question** ("Would
  it be ridiculous to…?", "Is it a bad time?"). A "no" makes them feel safe and
  in control.
- **You're about to deliver bad news or a big ask** → front-load a **softener /
  accusation audit** ("You're probably not going to like this…", "I probably
  sound like a pain here…"), then say the hard thing.
- **You must decline** → warm, firm **hard no** ("Your offer is generous — I'm
  sorry, that doesn't work for me"). No "but."

Default combo: **Label → (pause) → calibrated question.** Name the feeling,
let silence work, then hand them the problem to solve.

## Type the counterpart

Match your primary tool to who you're dealing with:

| Type | They care about | Lead with |
|---|---|---|
| **Assertive** | Being heard, winning, control | **Mirror.** And note: telling them to *do* something demotivates; telling them they *can't* motivates. "You're in charge here." |
| **Analyst** | Being right, precision, time | **Label.** Give them space and data; don't rush them. |
| **Accommodator** | The relationship | **Talk / rapport.** They'll agree to your face and stall later — draw out real concerns with calibrated questions. |

## Voice and delivery

- **Slow down. Pause between sentences.** Tempo signals control.
- **Upward inflection when mirroring** turns it into an invitation.
- **Late-night-FM-DJ voice** for hard moments: slow tempo, downward inflection,
  calm. It settles people.
- **Keep it light** — use humor when it's genuinely there, never forced.
- **Humanize yourself** (family, a small self-deprecating aside) to build trust.

## Anti-patterns — don't write these

| Don't say | Why | Instead |
|---|---|---|
| "Why…?" | Puts people on the defensive | A **label**, or a "what/how" question |
| "What do you mean by that?" | Sounds combative | "What's an example of __?" |
| "I understand." | Empty; they don't believe it | A **label** or a **paraphrase** |
| "You're right." | Means they've checked out | Aim for **"that's right"** via a summary |
| "But…" | Erases everything before it | Stop. Pause. Re-approach. |
