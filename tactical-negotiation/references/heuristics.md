# Heuristics

Situational if→then rules and reads. Load for diagnosis and edge cases. The
three or four most universal reads already live in SKILL.md; this is the long
tail.

## Reading the counterpart
- **If they're hammering on price, you're probably just a competing bid.**
  Reframe away from price toward value and fit — or find out who you're being
  compared against. ("What else would make this a good deal for you besides
  price?")
- **If someone is talking to you, they want something — you have leverage.**
  Engagement itself is information. Don't act desperate.
- **A sign you're being played:** the other side gets *angry* when you won't
  keep going with their pitch, or when you slow down. Genuine partners don't
  punish caution. Treat manufactured urgency and irritation as a tell.

## When a move stalls
- **Mirror falls flat (one-word reply)** — it's one of two things:
  - What they said felt too obvious to them. Surface it: "It seems like this is
    completely obvious to you."
  - They don't trust you yet. Name it: "It feels like I haven't earned your
    trust," or "It feels like you've really got your guard up."
- **They go silent after you ask** — let it sit. Silence is often them doing the
  work. Don't rescue them by talking.
- **They say "you're right"** — you've probably lost them; it's a polite exit.
  Back up and aim for a "that's right" by summarizing their view until they
  confirm it.

## Framing
- **Put yourself and the counterpart in the same in-group** — "we/us" against
  the problem, not you against them.
- **To promote *inactivity* (when the status quo favors you), ask vague,
  open-ended questions.** They overload and stall people.
- **Use I / you / we to bring out the "monkey" (emotion); use objective,
  third-party statements to bring out the "robot" (reason).** "I" and "you"
  create distance between people; objective statements create distance from the
  *problem* ("It seems like the market is setting this price, not either of us").
- **Anchor with an emotional pre-frame before a number or an ask** — "You're not
  gonna like this…" (pause) primes them to brace, so the actual ask feels
  smaller than feared.

## Watch-outs
- **Don't get hyper-focused on price or a single ask — implementation can kill
  you.** Protect the "how": the best price with an unworkable rollout is a loss.
- **Assertives are motivated by loss, not gain.** Frame what they'd *lose* by not
  moving, and give them the feeling of control.
- **Never end on the hard part.** Even a "no" conversation closes with warmth and
  an open door.
