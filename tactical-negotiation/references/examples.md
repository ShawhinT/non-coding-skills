# Worked Examples

Annotated before/after exchanges. Each shows the read, the move chosen, and the
phrasing. Use them as models to imitate — notice *why* the move was picked, then
adapt the wording to the user's voice.

---

**Example 1 — Pushback on price**

Counterpart: "This is way more than we budgeted. Your competitor is 30% cheaper."

Weak reply: "Well, our quality justifies the price because…" *(arguing → digs in)*

Strong reply:
> "It sounds like the value just isn't there for you at this number." *(label —
> drains the emotion, invites them to correct you)*
> (pause)
> "What would this need to look like for it to be an easy yes?" *(calibrated
> question — hands them the problem)*

Read: price-hammering means you may be a competing bid. Don't defend the number;
find the real constraint.

---

**Example 2 — Asking for a raise**

You want a bump; the manager opens guarded.

> "You've probably looked at a lot of numbers here, and it sounds like you
> already have a range in mind." *(label — surfaces their constraint without
> demanding it)*
> (let them respond)
> "How can I be involved in the projects that are most critical to the team this
> year?" *(calibrated question — reframes from 'pay me more' to 'make me
> indispensable')*

Read: making yourself needed changes the question from cost to investment.

---

**Example 3 — Declining an offer without wrecking the relationship**

They've made an offer you can't take.

> "This is genuinely generous, and I really respect how you've put it together."
> *(warmth / admiration audit)*
> "I'm sorry — it just doesn't work for me." *(hard no, no 'but')*
> (pause)
> "What am I missing that made this the right number on your end?" *(calibrated
> question — keeps the door open)*

Read: the "but" would erase the warmth. Separate the appreciation from the
decline with a full stop.

---

**Example 4 — They're venting / angry**

Counterpart: "Every time we work with a vendor it's the same — nobody listens
and we clean up the mess."

> "It sounds like this has been driving you crazy because no one ever actually
> listens." *(label for venting — let them feel heard)*
> (silence)
> "What would 'someone finally listening' look like on this project?"
> *(calibrated question — turns the vent into a spec)*

Read: don't defend or problem-solve first. Name the frustration, then convert it.

---

**Example 5 — Missing decision-makers**

On a call, the person can't actually say yes alone.

> "It feels like there are a few people not on this call who'll weigh in."
> *(label)*
> "How on board are they with where we've landed?" *(calibrated question —
> surfaces the real approval path)*
> "What do they see as the biggest challenge here?" *(arms your champion to sell
> it internally)*

Read: you're not closing them, you're equipping them to close the room you can't
see.

---

**Example 6 — Repairing blown trust**

Something went wrong and they've gone cold.

> "You feel like I've failed you." *(label — say the hard thing out loud)*
> (pause — let it be uncomfortable)
> "I'm sorry." *(clean, no excuse)*
> "What would I need to do to start making this right?" *(calibrated question)*

Read: naming the accusation before they do defuses it. Excuses re-inflate it.
