# LinkedIn DM campaign benchmarks

Funnel benchmarks for high-volume LinkedIn outreach campaigns (Sales-Navigator-style: connection request → first DM → reply → meeting). Use them when reviewing a campaign's health during a sync or status review, and to decide whether to keep split-testing a message or freeze it.

**Provenance:** these come from Uptown (a LinkedIn outbound agency; the same source as the "Uptown method" playbook in conversion-copy's `channel-outreach.md`), reporting across their client campaigns. They are external, directional numbers — not Shaw's data. As Shaw's own campaigns accumulate, replace or annotate these with his actuals; his numbers beat theirs.

## The funnel

| Stage | Benchmark | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Connection requests | 200/wk (LinkedIn's cap), ~800/mo | Sent blank, spread ~50/day Mon–Thu |
| Acceptance rate | 30%+ | The top-of-funnel cap — everything downstream is bounded by it |
| Reply rate (of messaged) | 20–30% | |
| Positive reply rate | 15–25% | |
| Positive reply → meeting | ~60% | Mostly a follow-up-discipline number, not a copy number |

Reference campaign shape (Uptown's pharmacy-tech client, ~6 months): ~3,000 requests → ~35% accepted → 219 replies → 49 positive → 24 meetings → 11 closed → ~$400k. Seller's own case study; treat as directional.

## How to read a campaign against these

- **Acceptance rate is upstream of everything.** If it's under ~30%, fix targeting (is the cohort filtered for *active* LinkedIn users — posted in the last 30 days?) or the profile's first impression (photo/headline), not the message. No message edit rescues a list that won't connect.
- **Accepted but silent → targeting or message.** If the cohort is confirmed right, it's the message — specifically the problem statement (the one variable slot in the Uptown template). Test a new thesis every 1–2 weeks; freeze once a variant lands in benchmark.
- **Replies but few positives → problem/offer mismatch.** The pain is landing but the mechanism or ask isn't. Revisit the "at a high level" line before touching the opener.
- **Positives but few meetings → follow-up gap, not copy.** Positive replies convert on persistence; check the tracker's `Next Contact` discipline before rewriting anything.

## Volume math (for pipeline planning)

At benchmark, ~800 requests/mo → ~240 accepts → ~50–70 replies → ~35–60 positives → ~20–35 meetings booked per month at full scale. Scale expectations down proportionally for smaller sends; a 50-person campaign produces single-digit meetings even when everything works.
