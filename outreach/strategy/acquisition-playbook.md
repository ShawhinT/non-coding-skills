# Acquisition Playbook

Strategy layer above campaign ops: which channels to work, where lists come from, and how message matches audience temperature. Read when planning a new campaign, choosing channels, or diagnosing "where do leads come from." Campaign mechanics (pipeline, enrichment, tracker) stay in SKILL.md — this file informs the *kickoff alignment* conversation, not the run.

## Core Four (Hormozi) — the complete channel map

Every way anyone ever learns you exist:

| | People who know you | Strangers |
|---|---|---|
| **1:1** | Warm outreach | Cold outreach |
| **1:many** | Content | Paid ads |

Use it as an audit at campaign kickoff: which quadrant is this campaign, and is it the right one? Shaw's default motion is warm outreach + content (per business-strategy's warm-before-cold principle); cold outreach is the expansion quadrant; paid ads currently unworked. When a campaign underperforms, check quadrant volume before quadrant choice — the usual failure is too little of a working channel, not the wrong channel (More, Better, New).

Warm outreach craft note: lead with relationship, not pitch — the ask is "who do you know who might need X," which takes the pressure off the contact themselves.

## Awareness pyramid (Eugene Schwartz) — match message to temperature

At any moment a market splits roughly: **3%** buying now, **17%** actively researching, **20%** problem-aware but not looking, **60%** unaware. Implications for campaign design:

- Everyone competes for the 3%; it's the most expensive, most crowded segment. Shaw's content/workshop/nurture motion exists to capture the 97% before they reach it.
- Segment scripts by temperature, not just relationship: a "buying now" contact gets the offer; a problem-aware contact gets the diagnosis or lead magnet; an unaware contact gets the problem named. Pitching an offer to the problem-aware is the classic mismatch — "asking someone to marry you on a first date."
- Free events and lead magnets are the bridge assets that move people up the pyramid; a campaign's CTA should sit one step ahead of the segment's temperature, not three.

## Dream 100 (Chet Holmes) — borrowed audiences

List the ~100 people, podcasts, newsletters, communities, and events that already aggregate the dream customer. Then work the list warmest-first: engage genuinely, guest, co-create, partner, sponsor. Trust transfers — an introduction from someone the audience already trusts skips months of cold nurture.

This is warm-before-cold applied to *distribution*: instead of building each audience from zero, borrow access. The lead-getter/repost campaigns in SKILL.md are Dream 100 executions; this frame also covers podcast guesting, workshop co-hosting, and partner channels. Maintaining an actual Dream 100 list (who, what audience, relationship status, last touch) makes it a system rather than opportunism.

## Own the traffic (Brunson)

Three kinds of traffic: **controlled** (paid — on/off switch, costs money), **earned** (viral, SEO, word of mouth — can't be relied on), **owned** (email list, community — reachable free, forever). Every campaign, whatever its immediate CTA, should push people toward owned: the Kit list is the asset, LinkedIn is rented land. Practical test at kickoff: does this campaign's funnel capture contact info somewhere, or does it terminate on a platform Shaw doesn't control?
