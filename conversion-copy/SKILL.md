# Conversion Copy

The canonical skill for copy whose job is conversion. The spine is Harry Dry's 3-question filter at the line level and seven principles at the piece level, plus one channel file loaded per format.

## The filter — every line passes all three

1. **Can I visualize it?** *"Worn by supermodels in London and dads in Ohio"* passes. *"Worn by diverse customers"* fails.
2. **Can I falsify it?** *"Cut monthly reporting from 6 hours to 20 minutes"* passes. *"Saves tons of time"* fails.
3. **Can nobody else say it?** If a competitor could sign the line — or the campaign concept with their name swapped in — it's noise, not copy.

Any no → rewrite. When diagnosing, score each key line ✓/✗ with a one-line reason. Never present copy that fails the filter without saying so.

## The seven principles

1. **Ground it in real customer language.** Copy written from imagination sounds like a brand wrote it. Mine testimonials, CRM notes, Reddit/LinkedIn comments, and Shaw's memory of client calls — he often has the sharpest line verbatim; ask him. Quote, don't translate: "drowning in busy work" stays as-is. Weight language by ICP fit — an off-target client's edit is a data point, not a veto.
2. **Write for one reader.** "You," not "users." Picture one specific customer; the right reader should think *that's literally me*. Default reader is the founder-CEO below.
3. **Sell the outcome, in specifics.** People buy the brighter smile, not the toothbrush. Every feature finishes "...so you can ___" or gets cut, and every claim carries a number, name, or timeframe. You can't bullshit specifics.
4. **Write how you talk.** Contractions, fragments, conjunctions opening sentences. Read it aloud; if it sounds written — or like a SaaS homepage — rewrite.
5. **Cut for density, not brevity.** Any word not working for you is working against you — but length isn't the enemy: for considered purchases, long copy with the facts outsells short (whoever reads 50 words reads 500). Cut words, never substance.
6. **Every line earns the next.** The first line is 80% of the job — and it doubles as the preview text, the pre-fold hook, or the headline depending on channel. Open with tension or specificity, never context. No paragraph should feel like an exit ramp.
7. **Show, don't push.** Proof next to every claim, artifacts over adjectives, advice frame over pitch frame — let the reader persuade themselves. (Exception: high-volume cold outreach deliberately trades this for testability — see the Uptown playbook in `references/channel-outreach.md`.)

## Non-negotiables

- **Verify proof claims before they ship.** Counts of people trained come from the ABA Trainings database (unique people; multi-session clients once, 2:1 sessions as two; exclude sessions that haven't happened). Wins and quotes come from the Testimonials database — Published/Approved only, and anonymity constraints extend to naming the company anywhere on the page. Round down: a "30+" that's true beats a "35" that isn't.
- **Find the researched fact before writing.** The Rolls-Royce headline came from an engineering report, not imagination. If the piece isn't built on a real fact (client result, session mechanic, before/after number), it's decoration.
- **No customer-language source handed over → ask before drafting.** If genuinely none exists (pre-launch offer), flag the guessed lines so Shaw knows what to pressure-test.
- **Draft 2–3 variants, each pointing at a different angle** (fear / dream / enemy), and state your top pick with reasoning. For headlines, generate wide (16+) internally, present the top 2–3.
- **Thin brief → push back.** Minimum: channel + offer + specific reader + customer-language source.
- **When copy works, run it until it stops selling.** Shaw will be bored of it long before the audience has seen it.

## Two modes

**Review** (Shaw pastes copy): confirm channel and reader → filter-score the key lines → flag the top 2–3 principle violations, not all seven → diagnosis first, then rewrites of the weakest lines. Don't silently rewrite; the diagnosis is the value.

**Draft** (Shaw briefs an offer): lock the brief → load the channel file → 2–3 variants with explicit angles → top pick with reasoning. Long-form goes section by section — lock the headline before the subhead.

## Propagate winners

When a line locks on one funnel surface, flag the sibling surfaces carrying the same promise (YouTube description CTA, event page, homepage hero, Calendly page) for the same update. One promise change everywhere at once keeps the voice coherent and A/B attribution clean.

## Shaw's voice

- Smart, no-fluff, peer-to-peer with busy skeptics. No hype, no hand-holding.
- **Kill landing-page words:** unleash, unlock, supercharge, empower, enhance, exceed, elevate, transform, revolutionary, game-changing, cutting-edge, next-gen (unless ironic), seamless, robust, leverage-as-a-verb.
- **Visceral verbs:** "delete 20 hours" beats "save 20 hours." Concrete one level deeper than feels necessary: "emails, briefs, follow-ups, decks," not "administrative work."
- **Light on em-dashes** — one or two per page; use commas, periods, parentheses.
- **Default ICP:** founder-CEOs of SMBs (3–200 employees) buying AI education. Runs a real team, still the bottleneck inside it. Not indie hackers, not enterprise.
