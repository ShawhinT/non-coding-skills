# Channel: Event Registration Pages (Luma)

Use this framework when writing or reviewing a registration page for a free live event — webinars, workshops, live trainings. Shaw hosts these on Luma under the AI Builder Academy calendar, so the page structure is fixed by the platform and the copy has to work inside it.

Event pages differ from landing pages in two ways: the ask is small (a free registration, not a purchase), and the platform supplies the chrome (date, host, register button). There is no subhead, no CTA copy to write, no pricing section. Everything rides on four blocks.

## Structure (fixed by the Luma pattern)

1. **Title** — doubles as the H1 and the social-share headline.
2. **What you'll learn** — exactly 3 bullets, each with the key phrase bolded.
3. **Why this matters** — 2–3 short paragraphs: name the enemy, show the proof, say what the session does.
4. **Who you'll learn from** — a 3–4 sentence bio adapted to this audience.

Work through these one at a time and lock each with Shaw before moving on. Don't one-shot the page.

## Title

- Series pattern: **"Claude [Code] for [Audience]: [Outcome]"** — e.g. *Claude Code for SWE Teams: Building a Shared AI Coding Toolkit*, *Claude for Executives: How to Delete 20 Hours of Busywork a Month*. Keeps the event catalog scannable and signals who it's for in the first three words.
- The outcome half must pass the central filter on its own: a falsifiable number ("20 hours a month") beats a theme ("work smarter").
- Visceral verb in the outcome. Shaw upgraded "delegate" to "delete" in the title — the title verb can be more aggressive than the body copy.

## What you'll learn

- Three bullets, each opening with an action verb, key phrase bolded mid-bullet (matches Luma's rendering of past events).
- Bullet 1 carries the unsignable angle + proof ("the same approach I've used with **30+ leaders** in 1:1 workshops").
- Bullets 2–3 name the concrete mechanics (tools, frameworks) with the "so that" attached ("so recurring tasks run themselves").
- Name the actual product features attendees will go looking for afterward (Cowork, skills, connectors) — and confirm the current product name with Shaw before publishing.

## Why this matters

- Paragraph 1: the enemy. The sharpest enemies are behavioral, not competitive ("most leaders still treat AI like a search bar").
- Paragraph 2: proof. Two quantified client wins beat five vague ones. Pull real numbers from the Testimonials database (see SKILL.md on verifying proof claims); keep anonymized wins anonymous ("One built skills that cut a 10-hour weekly newsletter to 1 hour").
- Paragraph 3: what the session does and what the reader leaves with ("you leave knowing which 20 hours of your month to hand off first").

## Who you'll learn from

- Re-cut the bio for each audience: lead with the credential *this* reader cares about. For executives that's "taught 30+ executives and senior leaders in 1:1 workshops"; for engineers it's the builder/YouTube credentials. Same facts, different order.
- The PhD and the 100k+ YouTube audience are supporting cast, not the lede, unless the audience is builders.

## Worked example (Claude for Executives, July 2026)

**Title:** Claude for Executives: How to Delete 20 Hours of Busywork a Month

**What you'll learn**
- Delegate work to Claude **like it's a new hire** — the same approach I've used with **30+ leaders** in 1:1 workshops
- Connect Claude to **the apps where your work actually lives** — email, calendar, docs — with Cowork
- Build **custom skills with a 4-step framework** so recurring tasks (briefings, reports, follow-ups) run themselves

**Why this matters**

You already know how to delegate. But most leaders still treat AI like a search bar: type a question, get an answer, do the real work themselves.

The leaders getting real time back treat Claude like a new hire instead. They give it context, connect it to their tools, and train it on their recurring work. One built skills that cut a 10-hour weekly newsletter to 1 hour. Another turned vendor assessments that took 1–2 hours each into 10–15 in 40 minutes.

In this session, I'll walk through the exact approach I use in my 1:1 workshops, so you leave knowing which 20 hours of your month to hand off first.

**Who you'll learn from**

Shaw Talebi is an AI educator and builder. He's taught 30+ executives and senior leaders to delegate work to Claude through his 1:1 workshops, and helps midsized companies roll out Claude to their teams and build AI-native operating systems. He earned his PhD from the University of Texas at Dallas, where he worked as an applied AI researcher, and teaches 100,000+ leaders, operators, and engineers on [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/@ShawhinTalebi).

## Audience-language notes for Shaw's exec events

- For the Claude-as-delegation metaphor, "new hire" is Shaw's locked choice — it landed better than "intern" (can read as beneath an exec's dignity to manage), "team member" (soft), or "assistant."
- Weight testimonial-language edits by ICP fit before treating them as gospel: a founder of a small startup editing a quote is weaker evidence about midsized-company leaders than a matching edit from an actual target reader.
