# Channel: Landing Pages

Standalone pages converting a visitor into a lead, trial, or buyer — offer pages, course pages, waitlist pages, homepages. The full 7-section sales-page skeleton belongs to `sales-letter-writer`; this file sharpens the page.

Lock before drafting: traffic type (cold vs warm), the single conversion action, reader's awareness level, customer-language source.

## Channel moves

1. **The title is a 30-second speed date.** Most visitors never return. Generate wide, then pick by the memory test: show candidates to people, ignore their advice, ask a day later which one they remember.
2. **Match awareness to traffic.** Warm (referral, newsletter) knows the problem — lead with the solution. Cold (search, blog) doesn't — lead with the problem. Ask Shaw which traffic the page gets.
3. **Lead with the unsignable angle.** Ladybird Driving School buried "all instructors are female" at row 4 — the reason people pick them. For Shaw it's usually "built for operators, not engineers" or "no code, no dev team."
4. **Speak with conviction.** "It's how," not "we help." "Replaces," not "alternative." Cut "might."
5. **Call-to-value buttons.** "Get my custom plan" beats "Submit." The CTA echoes the primary benefit and sounds like something the reader would say.
6. **Proof at objection points.** Testimonial about ROI after the price; about ease after the process — never one block at the bottom. Give each major objection ("no time," "too expensive," "AI hasn't worked for me") its own section.
7. **Scannable in 3 seconds per section.** "3 simple steps" converts better than two paragraphs. White space is free.

## Default skeleton

Title + subhead → who this is for → the promise (outcome, mechanism, timeframe) → social proof → how it works (3 steps) → what you get → guarantee → price + scarcity + CTA.

## Worked example — hero rewrite

> *Three months from now, your team automates the busy work you hate — no code, no dev team, no $60k SOW. You spend the saved hours doing what you started the business to do.*

Passes all three: visualize (the team automating, you doing the real work), falsify ("three months," "no code"), unsignable ("$60k SOW" names the competitor — bloated dev shops). It stacks: names the enemy, kills the objection, paints the outcome.
