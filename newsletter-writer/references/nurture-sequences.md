# Nurture Sequences (Kit automations, not broadcasts)

Two Brunson frameworks (DotCom Secrets) for **automated sequences** — what a new subscriber receives after opting in, or the emails between a lead magnet and an offer. This is situational material: read it only when building or auditing a Kit *sequence*; regular weekly broadcasts stay on SKILL.md's templates and never borrow from here. Shaw's voice rules (cold open, one thought per line, anti-hype, "Keep building,") apply unchanged — these frameworks govern sequence *architecture*, not sentence style.

## Soap Opera Sequence — the first 5 emails after opt-in

A serialized story across five sends, each opening a loop the next one closes. The point is training open behavior in the first week, when attention is highest, by making each email pull the next.

1. **Set the stage** — welcome, deliver whatever they opted in for, tell them what's coming, and open a dramatic loop ("tomorrow I'll tell you about the call that changed how I run my business...").
2. **High drama** — start at the worst moment of the backstory, then the wall: what was tried that didn't work. Relatable struggle, not credentials.
3. **The epiphany** — the breakthrough moment and the mechanism it revealed. This is where the reader meets the thing Shaw actually teaches/sells.
4. **Hidden benefits** — the second-order payoffs of the mechanism they wouldn't have guessed (for ABA: not just hours saved — the business becomes documentable, delegable, sellable).
5. **Urgency + CTA** — the one next step, with a real reason to act now. The only hard ask in the sequence.

Adaptation notes for Shaw: his origin material (physics PhD → Toyota → solo AI educator; "treating Claude like an intern" as epiphany) maps cleanly onto emails 2–3. Keep each email inside the newsletter length bands; a soap-opera email is still 250–450 words, not a chapter.

## Seinfeld emails — the ongoing rhythm after the sequence

Once the opening sequence completes, the list expects entertainment-first "emails about nothing": a daily-life story or observation that hooks, then hinges to a lesson connected to the core message, with a soft CTA. Priority order inside each send: entertaining first, educational second, promotional a distant third.

For Shaw this is not a new mode — his Observation template is already Seinfeld-shaped. The framework's addition is the *hinge* discipline: the story earns attention, the pivot connects it to the one core idea, and the CTA stays in the P.S. If the story doesn't hinge to anything, it's a diary entry; if the hinge comes first, it's a lecture. The jab-to-right-hook economy applies across sends: mostly give, occasionally ask.

## When to reach for this file

- Building a new Kit sequence behind a lead magnet or event opt-in
- Auditing why an existing sequence gets opens on email 1 and silence after
- Deciding what a new subscriber's first week should look like

Not for: weekly broadcasts, sell emails (→ SKILL.md sell mode + `conversion-copy`), or one-off replies (→ `email-helper`).
