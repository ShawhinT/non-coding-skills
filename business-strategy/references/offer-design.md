# Offer Design Playbook (Hormozi, $100M Offers)

The step-by-step build/audit process for any ABA offer — intensives, workshops, cohorts, productized services. The Value Equation in SKILL.md is the lens; this file is the mechanics. Use it in two modes: **build** (new offer from scratch) and **audit** (run an existing offer through each step and flag the weakest one). Audit is the more common mode — Shaw re-packages more often than he invents.

Downstream lanes: once the offer is designed, `sales-letter-writer` turns it into copy and owns the naming shortlist workflow; this file owns the design decisions themselves.

## Step 0 — Starving-crowd test (market before offer)

Before designing anything, check the market. All four must hold:

1. **Massive pain** — they desperately need it; degree of pain sets the ceiling on price. Sell water in the desert, not on a street corner.
2. **Purchasing power** — they can afford it or can access the money.
3. **Easy to target** — reachable through identifiable channels, communities, lists. A perfect market you can't reach is worthless.
4. **Growing** — a tailwind forgives mistakes; a shrinking market punishes everything.

Priority order: **market > offer > persuasion skill**. A weak market caps a great offer; no amount of copy saves a wrong crowd. If an offer is underperforming, check this step before polishing the stack — the fix might be who it's aimed at, not what's in it.

## Step 1 — Premium pricing posture

Two viable positions: lowest-price leader or high-value leader. There is one lowest-price slot and ABA isn't taking it. Pricing "a little less" than comparable offers is accidental commoditization — the goal is to be different enough that comparison fails (category of one). Premium prices also select for committed clients, who do the work and get the results that feed the proof flywheel. This restates SKILL.md's "value >> price, never discount" as a design input: set price from the value stack, not from competitors.

## Step 2 — Value-stack exercise (the core build)

Work from the customer's obstacles, not your deliverables:

1. **Name the dream outcome** vividly — what their work/life looks like after, not what they receive.
2. **List every problem** they hit *before* buying (what stops them starting), *during* (what makes them quit), and *after* (what threatens the result once achieved). Each problem taxes one of the four Value Equation levers.
3. **Flip each problem into a named solution.** "Meditating is boring" → "How to make meditation effortless." The solution list is the offer's raw material.
4. **Choose delivery vehicles** for each solution. Dimensions to vary: personal-attention level (1:1 / group / 1:many), customer effort (DIY / done-with-you / done-for-you), medium, format, speed. Two forcing questions:
   - **10x test:** what would you deliver if they paid 10x? (pulls toward high-touch)
   - **1/10th test:** how would you still overdeliver at a tenth of the price? (pulls toward templates, recordings, checklists — one-to-many assets with near-zero marginal cost, the highest-leverage components)
   The best stacks mix answers from both.
5. **Trim the stack.** Rate each component: value-to-customer × cost-to-you. Cut high-cost/low-value first, then low/low. The core is low-cost/high-value; reserve high-cost/high-value for premium tiers. This step is what keeps offers from becoming fulfillment nightmares — a component that survives trimming has earned its fulfillment burden.
6. **Present as named components.** A stack of named parts is perceived as worth more than the same content as one blob. Bonus components should collectively feel worth more than the core.

## Step 3 — Guarantee (pick from the taxonomy)

Ordered by how much risk the seller absorbs:

- **Unconditional** — full refund, no questions (30/60 days). Strongest trust signal, attracts some wrong buyers.
- **Conditional** — refund only if a specific result doesn't happen given specific client actions ("if X by day Y having done Z"). The workhorse for services; conditions keep it honest.
- **Stacked** — unconditional + conditional combined.
- **Anti-guarantee** — "all sales final" with a reason (exclusivity, capacity). A premium-positioning move, not an absence of guarantee.
- **Performance** — pay-on-results, milestones, rev share. Maximum alignment, maximum seller risk.

Design notes: name the guarantee (a named guarantee is quotable and memorable); set the guaranteed result at the *floor* of the promised range, not the average; and remember that buyers who purchase *because of* the guarantee are often the worst clients — the guarantee's job is to remove risk for good-fit buyers, not to recruit refund-seekers. High refund rates usually mean the guarantee attracted the wrong people.

## Step 4 — Scarcity & urgency (real only)

Scarcity = limited quantity (cohort caps, 1:1 capacity). Urgency = limited time (enrollment windows, price steps, expiring bonuses). Both must be true and tied to actual capacity — fake versions destroy trust permanently, and ABA's capacity constraints are real enough that invention is never necessary.

## Step 5 — Name it (MAGIC)

Magnet word + Avatar + Goal + Interval + Container: "The 6-Week Lean Body Accelerator for Busy Dads." A formulaic name that makes comparison-shopping impossible. The full naming workflow (shortlists by direction, container word library) lives in `sales-letter-writer` → `references/naming-playbook.md` — go there for the actual naming session; this line exists so naming isn't skipped in the design pass.

## Audit mode — running an existing offer through

Walk the steps in order and score each: market (step 0), price posture (1), stack (2 — especially: does every component survive the trim matrix? which Value Equation lever does each move?), guarantee (3), scarcity honesty (4), name (5). The weakest step is the constraint; fix that one, not everything. Cross-check against SKILL.md's ladder principle: an audit that concludes "add more rungs" is usually wrong — the ladder is learning scaffolding, and mature offers collapse it.
