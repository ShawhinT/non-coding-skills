# Event Promo Examples

Promos for Shaw's own live events (free webinars, workshops, launch sessions). A campaign runs ~3 posts with escalating directness: **announce → reframe → proof**. Early posts sell what the reader will learn; the final post proves it worked for peers and makes the hardest ask. All carry a direct CTA (unlike general promos in `examples-promo.md`), a real-asset image (event cover or screenshot, never a designed graphic), and a per-post UTM tag on the register link (`?utm_source=li1` / `li2` / `li3`) so registrations trace to the post.

## The pattern

**Sequence arc: announce → reframe → proof.** Post 1 is a value-framed announcement (what you'll be able to do, who it's for). Post 2 leads with the event's core thesis and buries logistics at the end. Post 3 is proof-dense insider access: credibility number + client wins + the hardest ask. Client success stories run as separate story posts *between* promos, so promo blurbs stay one-line teasers.

**Credibility-number open (proof post).** An earned, time-anchored number ("Over the past 3 months, I've taught 30+ leaders"), not an announcement. The event never appears in the first line of the late posts.

**The giveaway frame.** The event is positioned as free access to something normally paid and private ("exactly what I teach in those private 1:1 workshops"). Registering reads as arbitrage, not attending a webinar.

**One-line transformations, no mechanics.** Each client win is a single 🏆 line: before state, then after state. No skill names, no tool details, no how — the how is the event's content. If a blurb needs a second sentence to explain the mechanism, cut the mechanism.

**Status-rich anonymous attribution that mirrors the ICP.** Roles are senior and load-bearing (COO, marketing director, GTM leader) so the target reader sees themselves. Never IC-sounding titles for people who lead functions. Company descriptors stay loose enough to protect identity ("a cyber company") but keep the ICP flavor (VC firm, SaaS, cyber). Honor each client's permission level; for wins sourced from call notes rather than approved testimonials, loosen identifying descriptors further.

**Numbers do all the selling.** Every claim has a figure behind it (30+, 3 months, 20 hours, 1–2 hours → 10–15 in 40 minutes). Zero hype adjectives.

**Qualification pulled from the event page.** Each post names who it's for using the event page's own sharpest framing ("If you're a CXO or leader at a midsized company, this is made for you" / "busywork that doesn't make sense to delegate to a person"). The post sells the transformation; the landing page filters the clicker.

**Direct CTA + recording P.S.** Register link in every post, day and time explicit, UTM-tagged per post. The later posts close with "Can't make it? Register anyway and I'll send the recording" — it converts the undecided and captures the email either way.

**Image: real asset, not designed graphic.** Event cover or a screenshot (Luma page, actual work artifact). Screenshots of real work out-credential polished graphics; the cover can repeat across posts.
