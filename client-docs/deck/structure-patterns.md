# Structure patterns

The default proposal deck is three slides that mirror the A3 proposal logic an enterprise reader expects: state the purpose, prove it, then show the path. Each slide is "title-and-content" — no dense paragraphs. Extend only when the proposal genuinely needs it.

## Slide 1 — Executive summary

The job: in one glance, say what this is, why it matters, and what the buyer walks away with.

- **Purpose line** (lead with it, A3-style). One concrete sentence naming the audience and the outcome. Example shape: "Activate [client] leaders and champions with Claude, the starting point for a [client] Center of Excellence." Avoid abstractions; name the real goal.
- **What** — the mechanics of the offer (e.g. "90-minute 1:1 session, plus a 30-minute check-in" and the 4-step skill loop: do it once, distill into a skill, test, improve).
- **Why** — split by audience in a sentence each (e.g. executives get a clear mental model to set AI strategy; champions get a quick win that drives team adoption).
- **Outcomes** — the concrete things each participant leaves with (one working skill on a real workflow; the framework; 2-3 next skills from their own work). Pull these from the real session outline when one exists.

Layout that worked: a tinted purpose band across the top, then three cards (What / Why / Outcomes) with the Outcomes card lightly highlighted.

## Slide 2 — The impact

The credibility slide. Segment by the audiences in the room (commonly Executives / Non-technical champions / Technical champions) so each buyer sees themselves. See `impact-slide-patterns.md` for the content rules. Layout that worked: three cards, each with a colored audience icon, a short "Example use cases" or section label, and a few tagged items (Case study / Example).

## Slide 3 — Transformation roadmap

Shows the proposal is a journey, not a one-off — which sets up multi-session or retainer options without saying "buy more."

- Three staged cards: **Activation** (get set up, one real task), **Adoption** (delegate real work as a daily habit), **Expansion** (agents take on work at a scale and speed people couldn't reach before). Each card has a "What" and a "How we help" (the offering at that stage: 1:1 workshops → team workshops + office hours → ongoing use-case labs).
- An always-on **Foundation** bar beneath all three: accounts, controls, governance, ownership. Frame it as the layer you operate *within* (the client's CoE governance), evolving as adoption scales — not a prerequisite the client hasn't done. Use the warning-yellow treatment, not red (red reads as a problem; this is a reassurance).

## Common extensions (only when needed)

- **Project organization** — who owns what (e.g. partner owns the relationship and close; Shaw owns delivery and architecture). Useful when a partner subcontract or a buyer explicitly asks about roles. Decide with Shaw whether it's on the deck or kept verbal.
- **Scope / next steps** — a short closing slide when the proposal needs an explicit ask.
- **Pricing** — usually NOT on the customer-facing deck; it lives in the commercial/partner layer. Confirm before adding.

## Per-client conventions

Enterprise buyers in conservative groups ([Company]-style) value purpose-first structure, lots of whitespace, single-glance clarity, and sometimes a specified font. Honor those. The structure above already aligns with the A3 pattern (purpose → current/desired state → countermeasure → roadmap → follow-up).
