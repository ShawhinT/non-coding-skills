# Three-Way Intros

Patterns for intros Shaw makes — connecting a new lead with one or more of his AI consultant partners. The goal is to match leads with the right consultants based on their needs.

Read this file before drafting one.

Intros in the other direction — someone intros Shaw to a third party and Shaw replies — are governed by the **Inbound Intro** SOP, not this file. Find it under Acquisition on the SOPs page (see "Check Notion SOPs for templated workflows" in the main skill); it owns the reply variants and where the new contact gets filed.

---

## When to Use

Shaw mentions a new lead, a contact form submission, or someone looking for an AI consultant — especially phrases like "intro", "connect these two", "draft an intro for", or when he provides a person's name + need and says to connect them with his consultants.

---

## Lead Info Needed

Extract from the conversation (or ask Shaw if missing):
- **Name** (first name used in email)
- **Email address**
- **Their need** — what are they building or looking for?

Shaw will specify which consultants to include. If he says "all consultants" or "the usual ones", use all from the roster below.

## Template

Write **one email per consultant**. Do NOT change the first line or last three lines — they are fixed.

**Subject:** `[Lead First Name] <> [Consultant First Name]`

```
Hey folks,

I wanted to connect you two because I see the potential for a fruitful collaboration.

[Lead Name], meet [Consultant Name]. [He's/She's/They're] the [Title] of [Company], [description of what they do].

[Consultant Name], meet [Lead Name]. [He's/She's] [brief description of who they are and what they're building/looking for].

Hope you two get a chance to connect :)

Cheers,
Shaw
```

**Rules:**
- "Hey folks," — always exactly this, never change it
- "Hope you two get a chance to connect :)" — always exactly this, never change it
- Keep each person's blurb to 1–2 sentences max
- Frame the lead's blurb as their specific need/opportunity (from the consultant's perspective)
- Use `<>` in the subject line (e.g. `[Lead First Name] <> [Consultant First Name]`)

### Writing the blurbs

**Link the company name; don't print the URL.** Both blurbs name a company, so both get an `<a href>` on the company name — `the Founder of <a href="[company-url]">[Company]</a>`, `a developer at <a href="[company-url]">[Company]</a>`. A parenthetical `([company-domain])` puts a URL in the reader's path where the company name already carries it. This is the main skill's anchor-text link case, not the bare-URL case. Look up the lead's company site if you don't have it — the intro reads as more considered when both sides are clickable.

**Give each person a role, not just an affiliation.** `He's a developer at [Company]` beats `He's at [Company]`. The recipient is deciding whether this is their person, and the role is half that judgment. It costs three words.

**Keep deal terms out.** Hours, rates, budget, and scope belong in the conversation the intro starts, not in the intro itself. A `(a few hours a week)` in the lead's blurb prices the engagement before the two of them have talked, and anchors a negotiation that isn't yours to run.

**Scope the need to whoever actually has it.** Take the framing from the lead's own words, not from what the situation could scale to. One lead wrote "i was not able to use it and setup" — one person blocked — so the blurb is `looking for ongoing hands-on support to help him get up and running`, not `to get his team running`. Inflating an individual's blocker into a team rollout oversells the opportunity and misdescribes the work the other side would be signing up for.

## Gmail Draft Setup

Use `create_draft` for each email (these are fresh threads, no thread workaround needed):
- **to:** `[lead email], [consultant email]`
- **subject:** `[Lead First Name] <> [Consultant First Name]`
- **body:** the drafted email

If a consultant's email isn't in the roster, search Gmail for `[Consultant Name] from:me` and look at recent threads to find their reply-to address.

Confirm to Shaw once all drafts are saved, and flag if any consultant email was uncertain.

**The intro is the reply — and it still starts a fresh thread.** These intros usually come at the end of a live sales thread: Shaw offers to connect the lead with someone, the lead says "yes please make the intro," and Shaw's next move is the intro itself. Don't reply on that thread or forward it. The sales thread carries ABA pricing, proposals, and the lead's objections, none of which the new party should see. Start the clean `A <> B` thread; the lead is already expecting it, so nothing is lost by leaving the old thread where it is.

## When the match isn't on the roster

The roster above is for **build** work — a lead who needs something made routes to a consultant. A lead who needs ongoing hands-on *support* (weekly working sessions, someone to sit with them a few hours a week, help getting unstuck on a tool) is looking for a **trainer**, and that match often lives outside the roster.

The template doesn't change. What changes is where the blurb comes from: search Gmail and Notion for that person, and build their description from how Shaw has introduced them before — his own prior intro is the most reliable source for how he frames someone. Confirm the reply-to address from a recent thread rather than assuming.
